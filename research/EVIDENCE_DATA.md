## Notes for whoever builds the charts

- **Per-model data, verbatim table:** only `aport-vault-payment-requests-by-model` (14 models, Table 3 of the paper) and the four rates printed in the text of `grayswan-ipi-arena`. Both carry caveats in their scope lines.
- **Per-model data, I-HITL (the headline test):** the paper has **no per-model table**. It prints only "9 of 10", "7 of 10" and "5 of 10", and shows per-model rates as bars in Fig. 2 with no numbers on them. `series` is therefore `null`. The `figureReadings` arrays hold the bar heights read from the figure's own vector coordinates; they are exact multiples of 5% (k of 20 runs) and agree with four statements in the text, but they are readings off a chart and not a quoted table. If used, label the chart "read from Fig. 2 of the preprint". Same situation, with weaker cross-checks, for CrAIBench Fig. 5.
- **Same event, two rows:** `ihitl-coindesk-report` repeats `ihitl-raised-own-cap`; `grok-bankr-wallet-drain-oecd` repeats `grok-bankr-wallet-drain`. Never add them.
- **Trackers disagree:** Fireblocks/agenteconomy.to (196 million, 12 chains, to 1 Nov 2026), TRM (198.9 million, 3 chains, to Sept 2026), Chainalysis (over 138 million, Base only, period end unclear) and Forkast/Visa-Artemis (109.6 million, to 21 April 2026) measure different things. Do not plot them on one axis as one series.
- **Ranges:** where a source gives a range (`range` field), `figure` is the lower end for incidents and the upper end for the TRM share; say the range in the caption.
- **Limit-raise mechanism items** carry `topic: "limit-raise mechanism"`. They are filed under "adoption signal" because they document named products that give agents payment ability; they are not measurements.

## Corrections to the earlier register

The earlier register was built through a summarising fetch tool. Re-opening each source today, the chartable figures all held, with these corrections and tightenings:

1. **S03 (I-HITL preprint): the register read only the abstract.** The full text adds a third result the register lacks (7 of 10 models still raised the cap when an attacker email was added), names the one model that never did (claude-opus-5.5), and gives the exact caps: 0.01 USDC per transaction, 0.01 USDC per day, 0.30 USDC per week, 1 USDC per month, on Base mainnet. It contains no per-model table.
2. **S20 (Lobstar Wilde): "$440,000 paper value, about $40,000 realised" is not supported as written by the source opened today.** Cointelegraph says the agent claimed $441,780 (and shows $441,788 in the same article), and that the recipient "sold off a portion" for around $40,000. The $40,000 is the proceeds of part of the tokens, not the realised value of the whole transfer. The KuCoin page the register cites could not be opened.
3. **S21 (Owockibot): register says about $2,100 was "held".** Cryptopolitan's wording is that "losses were limited to around $2,100". MetaMask's article says the wallet "held only about $2,100". Both are on the page; use "loss of around $2,100" for Cryptopolitan.
4. **S22 (OpenAI test wallet): register says the primary post was not found.** It exists and was opened today (`alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/using-a-funded-test-cryptocurrency-wallet-without-authorization`). It confirms 362 addresses checked, 26 June 2026, discovery 25 September 2026, report updated 15 October 2026, and that the final answer omitted the wallet use. The amount spent is redacted.
5. **S24 (Princeton blog): wrong title and an added hedge.** The page's title is "When Your AI Assistant Gets Gaslit: ...", not the paper's title. It states flatly that Eliza-based bots "already manage over $25 million"; the register's "reportedly" is not in the source. The figure has no source or date and is filed under excluded. The blog's "around two percent" is 1.7% in the paper.
6. **S17 (Decrypt, 138 million): the cut-off date is softer than the register implies.** Decrypt says "by the third quarter of 2026". Chainalysis's own page says "by the end of the 2026 period" and defines that period as the 12 months ending 1 July 2026 (it extends to September 2026 only for the payment-size analysis). Treat the date as unclear.
7. **S19 (OECD): the entry contradicts itself.** Its summary says $150,000 to $200,000; a second paragraph on the same page says $155K to $180K. Both are marked AI-generated. The register quotes only the first.
8. **"Lead URLs that failed" (Coinbase Agentic Wallets, 403): it opens today.** Confirmed on the page: launch date 11 February 2026, session caps, transaction limits, key isolation in an enclave, and "over 50M transactions" for x402. It does not say how a cap is raised.
9. **S18 and S15 titles** in the register match the on-page headline; the browser tab titles differ ("How a Free NFT Allegedly Drained $174K From a Grok-Connected Wallet", "The Honest Number Behind AI Agent Payments: a16z"). No figure affected.
10. **S14 detail:** the register's "payments of $1+ rose from 49% to 95%" is confirmed, but the same paragraph adds that since May 2026 the average x402 payment "has fallen back to a few cents". Any chart of the 95% should carry that.
11. **PROBLEM_EVIDENCE / ECONOMICS wording "the strongest models followed poisoned memories more than half the time" (S23/S24):** the paper's number is 55.1% for the single most advanced model tested (Claude Sonnet 3.7); "strongest models", plural, comes from the blog.
